Hi, I'm Farid Maleki. My academic background is in telecommunications and telecom systems design. But after about seven years working in that field, I realized where my real interest lay: automating processes, building intelligent systems, and detecting patterns using AI and machine learning.

So I taught myself — through the growing pool of online courses and my own research — and steadily built expertise in this new direction. My first real foray was research into speech emotion recognition, and from there I moved into time-series analysis, which became the formal start of my AI career at Rightel.

At Rightel, I studied how the performance department evaluated RAN and Core network health, and saw an opportunity: apply machine learning to time-series data to optimize performance and detect anomalies. I ended up delivering six products there — four for the RAN layer and two for the Core layer — all built around time-series-based anomaly detection.

From there, I carried that expertise into healthcare, building a RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) platform for Eastern Pharma in Australia. The system searched licensed medical literature and open-access research to surface evidence for each pharmaceutical ingredient — what indications it supported and what evidence backed that claim — and compiled it into the standardized evidence-package documentation required by Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA), a mandatory step for bringing a product to market there.

After that, I joined Procycons, a company working on lifecycle assessment. My job was to build a robust parser capable of ingesting the wide range of lifecycle-assessment reports the company needed to process and feed into their product. The months I spent there taught me a great deal — about clean coding practices, sound software engineering, and how a team should actually work together.

Next, I joined Robin, where I took on two main missions. The first was optimizing their existing Elasticsearch-based matching system — adding semantic search, sentiment analysis, vector search, and sparse models. The second was building a recommendation system on top of that, driven by user feedback. Elasticsearch turned out not to handle this well without significant added complexity, so we migrated both search and recommendations to Vespa AI — tapping into GPU acceleration for speed while properly supporting the recommendation layer. Alongside this, we also built an industry-focused chatbot that helped recruiters complete job postings and matched them with suitable candidates.

I then joined MCI, Iran's largest telecom operator. Between the years of experience I'd built and my original telecom background, I found myself in a strong position to take on more ambitious projects — suddenly working with a massive volume of data critical to the company's business goals. One project picked up where I'd left off at Rightel: continuing the anomaly-detection work I'd started there. Another was newer: with the recent progress in LLMs, RAG systems, and speech processing (ASR and TTS), I built and shipped a production AI call center — replacing human call-center agents with an AI agent capable of handling multiple conversations simultaneously, cutting the team's workload significantly while maintaining high accuracy and fast response times.

Alongside all of this, I've worked deliberately on my management skills — leading teams, fostering real collaboration between people, and learning to read each person's character so I can put them where they'll do their best work. I mentor regularly through ADPlist now, and I've mentored people from many different countries over the years — most of whom, I'm glad to say, reached a genuinely good place in their careers. I'm someone who loves learning as much as I love teaching; I get real satisfaction from helping people and seeing them succeed. Teammates have often told me I'm the one who keeps the team's energy up — we joke and laugh, but we also take the work seriously, and I hold myself to a high standard on milestones: if I've committed to a deadline, I deliver. That's built genuinely good relationships with every manager I've worked with, and real value for the employers I've partnered with over the years.

Right now, I'm working on a range of interesting projects, and I'm genuinely enjoying where I am. I keep learning, I keep teaching, I keep shipping good work — and I get real satisfaction from the path I'm on.
